Why these three go together

Water hits the wall, runs down the siding, and is supposed to leave through the gutter. Paint is the layer that keeps the siding from drinking any of it. Repaint over soft boards and you have bought yourself two years. Replace the siding but leave the gutter pitched the wrong way and the same corner rots again. Doing them in one scope costs less than doing them three times.

  • Siding installation and repair — rot cut out, new boards in, sealed and finished on a straight line.
  • Gutter installation and replacement — seamless gutters, downspouts and correct discharge away from the foundation.
  • Gutter repair — re-pitching, re-hanging, sealing joints and fixing fascia damage behind them.
  • Exterior painting — prep, patch, prime, paint. The prep is the part that decides how long it lasts.

The prep nobody sees

Exterior paint in Los Angeles fails from ultraviolet on the south face and moisture at the bottom of the wall. Both are prep problems before they are paint problems: caulk lines that were never cut out, chalked surfaces that were never washed, bare wood that was never primed. A&P prices the prep as part of the job rather than treating it as an optional extra.

Fascia, trim and the edges

The gutter hangs off the fascia, the fascia sits behind the drip edge, and the drip edge belongs to the roof. That handoff is exactly where a siding contractor and a roofer usually blame each other. Because A&P does both, it is one line item and one crew.
